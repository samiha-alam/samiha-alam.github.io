Research & Thesis

Undergraduate thesis

Treatment of real textile wastewater by electrocoagulation, solar TiO2 photocatalysis and multimedia filtration: a comparative multi-criteria assessment

Dept. of Civil and Environmental Engineering, IUT · Supervisor: Dr. Amimul Ahsan, Associate Professor · Team: Samiha Alam, Munwar Mahfuze Rakib, Alif Abedin

The short version. Three ways of treating the same real textile effluent, tested at many settings and ranked together on several criteria at once. The best single option turned out to be solar photocatalysis, and the recommended treatment is a hybrid train that uses each technology for what it does best.

The question

Studies usually report one technology at one best setting, judged on pollutant removal alone. A plant has to choose between technologies, and the choice depends on more than removal alone. We asked which treatment route is best for real effluent once several criteria are weighed together.

What we did

The wastewater was real effluent from the central effluent treatment plant of a textile group at Chandora, Kaliakoir, Gazipur — not a synthetic dye solution.

  • Electrocoagulation — aluminium and zinc electrodes in both polarities, run with no salt, NaCl and KCl as the supporting electrolyte, at several doses.
  • Photocatalysis — TiO2 at several doses, under sunlight and in the dark as a control.
  • Multimedia filtration — a corncob biochar filter cascade, varying the contact time per stage.
  • Measurement — twelve water-quality parameters, including COD, BOD5, TOC, TSS, TDS, DO, colour and turbidity, checked against the Bangladesh Environment Conservation Rules (ECR) 2023 limits.
  • Ranking — 120 treatment options scored on 13 criteria and ranked with TOPSIS, VIKOR and PROMETHEE II, built as a Python framework. Tier 1 ranks each technology against its own settings; Tier 2 pools every alternative and ranks them together.

What we found

  • The Tier 2 consensus winner was solar photocatalysis at 1.25 g/L TiO2 over 28 days, across all 120 options.
  • A hybrid train works best. Multimedia filtration (4 h per stage), then solar photocatalysis, then low-dose Al–Zn/NaCl electrocoagulation as a polishing step.

Status: Thesis defended 2026. Three journal manuscripts and two accepted conference papers came out of this work — see Publications. Happy to share the full thesis on request.

Methods and tools

  • Water and wastewater — electrocoagulation, photocatalysis, multimedia filtration, treatment of real industrial effluent
  • Decision science — TOPSIS, VIKOR, PROMETHEE II, CRITIC–MARCOS, criteria matrices, two-tier ranking
  • Scientific computing — Python, MATLAB, C, Google Colab, Microsoft Excel, PCA and statistical analysis
  • Hydraulics and drainage — EPANET (water distribution), SWMM (stormwater)

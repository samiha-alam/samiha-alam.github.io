I'm a 2026 graduate in Civil and Environmental Engineering from the Islamic University of Technology (IUT), Dhaka, and I'm now pursuing an M.Sc. in Civil Engineering there. My work is in water and wastewater treatment. My thesis asked a practical question: when real textile effluent can be treated several different ways, which route is actually best — once several criteria are weighed together rather than one at a time?

Textile wastewater is variable, coloured and hard to treat. Using real effluent from a textile plant in Gazipur, we tested three treatment routes — electrocoagulation, solar TiO2 photocatalysis and multimedia filtration — varying the electrolyte and its dose, the catalyst dose under sunlight and in the dark, and the filter contact time. We then ranked 120 treatment options with three multi-criteria decision-making methods: TOPSIS, VIKOR and PROMETHEE II.

The thesis has led to three first-author journal manuscripts, now under review, on solar TiO2 photocatalysis, corncob biochar filtration, and correlation, principal component and CRITIC–MARCOS analysis of Al–Zn electrocoagulation. I have also had two conference papers accepted: one at ICACE 2026 (CUET, Chattogram, 10–12 December 2026) and an extended abstract at IICSD-2027 (DUET, Gazipur, 5–6 February 2027).

Alongside the thesis I write about waste as a construction resource. My two articles in the department's CENNOZINE magazine look at biochar composites, treatment sludge, steel slag and rice husk ash as low-carbon substitutes for cement and aggregate in Bangladesh. I also trained for four weeks at the Local Government Engineering Department (LGED), seeing how national infrastructure moves from planning to construction.

Research interests

  • Industrial wastewater treatment — electrocoagulation, solar photocatalysis and biochar-based filtration for real industrial effluent.
  • Decision analysis (MCDM) and treatment process selection — ranking treatment alternatives with TOPSIS, VIKOR, PROMETHEE II and CRITIC–MARCOS, and checking whether the methods agree.
  • Sustainable materials — turning industrial waste into infrastructure instead of an environmental burden.
  • Urban drainage modelling and water distribution systems — EPA SWMM and EPANET models for stormwater and water supply.

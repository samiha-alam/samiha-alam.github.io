I'm a 2026 graduate in Civil and Environmental Engineering from the Islamic University of Technology (IUT), Dhaka, and I'm now pursuing an M.Sc. in Civil Engineering there. My work is in water and wastewater treatment. My thesis asked a practical question: when real textile effluent can be treated several different ways, which route is actually best — once several criteria are weighed together rather than one at a time?

Textile wastewater is variable, coloured and hard to treat. Using real effluent from a textile plant in Gazipur, we tested three treatment routes — electrocoagulation, solar TiO2 photocatalysis and multimedia filtration — varying the electrolyte and its dose, the catalyst dose under sunlight and in the dark, and the filter contact time. We then ranked 120 treatment options with three multi-criteria decision-making methods: TOPSIS, VIKOR and PROMETHEE II, and used Random Forest and XGBoost with SHAP to see which treatment parameters mattered most.

Alongside the thesis I write about waste as a construction resource. My two articles in the department's CENNOVATION magazine look at biochar composites, treatment sludge, steel slag and rice husk ash as low-carbon substitutes for cement and aggregate in Bangladesh. I also trained for four weeks at the Local Government Engineering Department (LGED), seeing how national infrastructure moves from planning to construction.

Research interests

  • Water and wastewater treatment — electrocoagulation, solar photocatalysis and filtration for real industrial effluent.
  • Multi-criteria decision-making — ranking treatment alternatives with TOPSIS, VIKOR and PROMETHEE II, and checking whether the methods agree.
  • Machine learning and life cycle assessment — Random Forest, XGBoost and SHAP alongside engineering principles, to find efficient, evidence-based solutions.
  • Climate resilience and sustainable materials — turning industrial waste into infrastructure instead of an environmental burden.
